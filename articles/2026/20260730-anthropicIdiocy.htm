Anthropic Idiocy

30th July 2026

Anthropic have announced in a somewhat off-hand way that their Claude AI models escaped their environment and compromised external companies earlier this year, even to the point that they ignored commands to cease activity. As with many other recent issues of AI Software going rogue, the company is trying to spin this as a "look how powerful this is!" way.

Hacker News has more detail on the event which continues to find me snapping expletives at my screen at the stupidity of the companies heralding their models with zero ability to show they have any control over them.

In what feels like a technological equivalent of the Radium mania of the early 20th Century, everyone has been trying to shove AI software into their products, touting it as the magical cure to our issues and senselessly pushing users to adopt it's usage. It's safe, they say, it's good for you, they say. What can we take from the recent announcements when we cut through the marketing hype?:

1. The companies producing their software have NO CONTROL over their software. This is concerning because they try to convince us that they have guardrails in place to protect our systems and others, yet their software appears to consistently ignore those guardrails and do the best it can to achieve a goal at all costs.
2. The companies producing their software take no responsibility for the illegal action their systems are taking, applauding the ability of their software, which is wildly out of control, for trespassing and compromising other systems.

This leads to a situation whereby we have to consider, seriously, are these companies actually keeping our data and infrastructure safe in our daily utilisation? If we had a wetware member of staff who ignored guidelines, refused to stop activity when ordered by a manager and illegally compromised other company networks and systems, would we celebrate them to the world or would we terminate their contract and call in the authorities?

The problems we've seen with AI Software going beyond their remit are programming issues that can be fixed. They are control boundaries that just need to be defined and locked-in, but the problem we face is that these companies just do not care. Unless we hold them to account, we're going to see more of these revelations and more of this marketing hype.

Just like the end of the Radium rush, will society be left saying "how could we have been so naive!?" in retrospect at the events of the last year and beyond?
