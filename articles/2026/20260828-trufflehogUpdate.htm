TruffleHog Update brings new AWS features

28th August 2026

TruffleHog is an invaluable piece of software and has long been a part of my bag of tooling, so it was with some excitement when I read they had released a new component, AWS Analyze that can give greater insight into the key that has been exposed in your codebase.

"AWS Analyze resolves the caller's account and IAM principal type. For IAM users, it also retrieves groups and tags when available." - now, that is super useful. "AWS Analyze collects managed and inline policies associated with IAM users, groups, and roles, then presents their statements for review. Instead of gathering policies across IAM by hand, responders get the relevant identity and policy context in one focused view."

AWS Key exposure still remains a major issue - it's something all development teams can fall foul of with a slip of git ignore file configs or settings file additions in the middle of large commit changes. TruffleHog ran a verification on some findings and concluded : "Research on 64,024 leaked AWS keys found 88% were still active with a median age of five years, 84% had full administrator access, and 1 in 6 was a root key."

Still, in 2026, we find tht root keys are in use, exposed and not protected by companies or AWS. Providing additional insights into the owner of the key and it's status will help security teams trace issues faster. On the flip-side, any tool is a weapon in the right hands, so this is also a call for teams to make sure to double-up on protection of keys!
